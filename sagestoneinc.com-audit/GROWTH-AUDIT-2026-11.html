OverviewSummary

Search visibility is fixed since the July audit. The site now prerenders every page, has unique titles and descriptions, a sitemap, schema on 39 of 42 pages, nine long-form blog posts, and SEO landing pages for industries and solutions. That foundation is good.

The gaps are now commercial. Nothing on the site tracks a conversion, so paid media cannot be run or measured. "Book a Call" opens a form rather than a calendar, which adds up to a day before anyone talks. The homepage and case studies still use testimonials and stats that cannot be verified, and the site gives buyers no price signal in a category where prices are usually published.

Proof & positioningMarketing

  • CriticalTestimonials look fabricated

    "Elena Marsh, Northline Studio", "David Okafor, Meridian Commerce" and "Priya Nair, Cedar & Co." appear on the homepage and the case studies page. The July audit flagged them as invented, and they are still in src/app/content/site.ts. The FTC's 2024 rule on consumer reviews and testimonials prohibits fake testimonials, so this is a legal risk as well as a trust problem.

    Remove them now. Replace them with real quotes (first name, role, company, and a LinkedIn link where the client agrees) or a single founder note until real quotes exist.

  • CriticalStats can't be verified and contradict each other

    "98% client retention" and "150+ growth-focused teams supported" show on the homepage and About page with no source. "24h average onboarding start" sits next to "24–72 hours" on How It Works and in the FAQ.

    Use only numbers you can back up from your own records, and state the window once ("onboarding starts within 72 hours"). If the client count is small, show named logos instead of a count.

  • HighThree of four case studies are anonymous outcomes

    "Creative Agency: 31% more billable capacity", "E-commerce: response time cut in half" and "Professional Services: cycle time reduced 22%" have no client name and no detail page. The one full case study, Kuha, shows go-to-market and content work rather than the VA and support services the site sells.

    Write two case studies on core services using the Kuha page template: client, starting problem, the role placed, the first 30 days, a metric, and a quote.

  • HighThe Philippines advantage is hidden

    The homepage H1 ("Structured support for businesses built to grow.") and the hero copy never mention the Philippines, cost, or the time-zone overlap. Those are the main reasons a U.S. buyer is looking at this category. The word appears only in the nav.

    Make the hero say who, what and why in one line, for example "Dedicated Filipino assistants and support staff for U.S. teams, working your hours from day one."

  • MediumBook a call is the only conversion

    Nine blog posts with 1,000 to 1,250 words each draw informational traffic, but there is no email capture, checklist download or newsletter. Readers who are not ready to book leave without a way to come back.

    Add one lead magnet tied to the existing posts, such as a "Delegation Task List" PDF or a "VA onboarding checklist", and nurture with a five-email sequence.

  • MediumThe brand name is written two ways

    The privacy policy, SMS terms, SMS consent text, footer logo text and those pages' titles use "Sage Stone". Everywhere else the site uses "SageStone".

    Keep "Sage Stone" in the SMS consent text and SMS terms if that is the brand name registered for A2P 10DLC, because carriers check that it matches exactly. Elsewhere, standardize on "SageStone", and confirm whether the legal name is "SageStone Inc." or "Sage Stone Inc." (both appear).

  • MediumTwo page titles are thin

    Correction: Organization and WebSite JSON-LD are already in index.html on every page. An earlier draft of this report missed them because of how the crawler matched the JSON. /faq has the title "FAQ | SageStone" (15 characters) and /case-studies has "Case Studies | SageStone" (24 characters). No llms.txt is published.

    Add a contactPoint to the existing Organization schema, publish an llms.txt, and retitle the two pages, for example "Virtual Assistant & Outsourcing FAQ | SageStone".

  • KeepWhat's working

    The voice is calm and specific. Service, industry and solution pages are keyword-led and interlinked, every URL in the sitemap returns 200, unknown URLs return a real 404, and the blog posts use BlogPosting schema.

  • Critical"Book a Call" doesn't book a call

    Every CTA leads to a seven-field form that promises a reply "within one business day". The buyer has to wait, then go back and forth over email to pick a time. Each step loses leads who were ready to talk.

    After the form is submitted, show a Cal.com booking page in place of the "Thank you" message and put the same link in the confirmation email. Keep the form fields so you still get context before the call.

  • HighNo pricing signal

    The site never mentions cost, hours, or engagement models. Buyers in this category compare hourly or monthly rates before they book anything. Without a range, price shoppers book calls that go nowhere and serious buyers leave to find a number elsewhere.

    Publish a /pricing page with "from" pricing for part-time and full-time dedicated roles, what's included (SageStone oversight, replacement guarantee, onboarding), and the minimum commitment.

  • HighThe form doesn't qualify leads

    The form asks for a service and a free-text message only. There is nothing on hours needed, start date or team size, so the first call has to cover the basics.

    Add two dropdowns: "Hours per week" (part-time, full-time, not sure) and "When do you need someone?" (this month, 1–3 months, exploring). Use the answers to route and prioritize.

  • HighLeads go to an inbox only

    api/contact.js sends a ZeptoMail notification and an auto-reply. It doesn't create a CRM record, store the source page or UTMs, or start a follow-up sequence.

    Also post each submission to Zoho CRM (or Bigin) as a Lead, with the source URL, UTM fields and the qualifying answers. Set up a two-touch follow-up for leads who don't book within 24 hours.

  • MediumNo way to see the talent

    Rung one of the belief ladder in PRODUCT.md is "These are real, senior, dependable people." The site never shows one.

    Add three to six anonymized "sample talent" cards with first name, years of experience, tools, past roles, and a short intro video where possible. This is the single most persuasive asset in the VA category.

  • MediumNo guarantee or risk reversal

    The site never says what happens if the match doesn't work out.

    State the replacement policy in plain words, for example "If the fit isn't right in the first 30 days, we rematch at no cost", and repeat it next to every CTA.

Before and after the saleSupport

  • HighA support company with no support channel

    There is no chat, no help widget, and no published hours for the phone number +1 (214) 945-2234. For a company selling customer support outsourcing, the website itself is the first chance to show what good support feels like.

    Add a Tidio chat widget, staffed during U.S. business hours with an AI first-responder trained on the FAQ after hours. Show response hours next to the phone number.

  • HighThe FAQ is thin

    The /faq page has about 390 words. It doesn't answer the questions buyers ask most: cost, contract length, minimum hours, what happens if the assistant leaves or is sick, time-zone coverage, data security and NDAs, how billing works, and whether there's a trial.

    Grow it to 20–25 questions grouped by Pricing, Hiring and matching, Working together, and Security. Each answer also feeds FAQPage schema and AI search answers.

  • MediumExisting clients have no path

    Nothing on the site tells an existing client how to raise an issue, reach their account manager, or request a change.

    Add an "Existing client? Get help" link in the footer and on the contact page. It should point to a support email or form with a stated response time and an escalation contact.

  • MediumNo business address

    The address shown is "Remote-first · Global". A registered business address builds trust with U.S. buyers, completes Organization schema, and is expected for SMS (10DLC) brand registration, which the site's SMS terms imply is in use.

    Show the registered U.S. business address (or a virtual office) in the footer and on the contact page.

Tracking & channelsPaid media

The site can't run measurable ads in its current state. On every page tested, the only third-party hosts loaded were Google Fonts and Unsplash. The only analytics is Vercel Analytics, which counts page views but no conversions.

  • CriticalNo conversion tracking

    A form submission swaps in a "Thank you" message on the same URL, so there is no thank-you page to use as a goal, and no event fires.

    Install GTM, then load GA4, the Google Ads tag, Meta Pixel and the LinkedIn Insight Tag through it, with a consent banner. Fire generate_lead on a successful submit and redirect to /contact/thank-you. Add Meta Conversions API from the Vercel function so the lead is still counted when browser tracking is blocked.

  • HighNo landing pages built for ads

    Every page carries the full header nav, seven nav links, and a footer with six link columns. Paid traffic needs one message and one action.

    Build /lp/hire-a-virtual-assistant and /lp/customer-support-outsourcing, set to noindex: logo only, a headline that matches the ad, pricing from-rate, talent cards, one case study, the guarantee, and an inline form with a scheduler.

  • HighNo lead source captured

    The form doesn't save UTMs, gclid, fbclid or the landing page URL, so a booked call can't be traced to the campaign that produced it.

    Save UTMs and click IDs to sessionStorage on arrival, send them as hidden fields, and write them to the CRM lead.

  • HighMobile menu button is cut off

    At 390px width (most current iPhones) the menu button runs 15px past the screen edge and is partly hidden.

    Below 420px, move the theme toggle into the menu and shorten the header button to "Book".

  • HighPrimary CTA fails contrast

    Ivory text on sage measures 3.42:1. WCAG AA needs 4.5:1 for text of this size, and the product brief targets AA.

    Use the existing --sage-ink (#59634F) as the button background. With ivory text it passes at about 6:1.

  • MediumSmall layout shift on mobile

    The homepage on a phone measured CLS 0.109, just above the 0.1 "good" threshold. Correction: an earlier draft reported a 775 KB font download on phones. That came from the test's shortened browser ID, which made Google Fonts send older TTF files. Real iPhones receive WOFF2 at a size similar to desktop.

    Recheck CLS with real-device field data from Vercel Speed Insights before changing anything; it may also have been inflated by the TTF fonts in the test.

  • MediumHotlinked stock images

    The service pages still load images from images.unsplash.com, and site.ts defines Unsplash stock portraits.

    Replace them with real team photos, self-hosted as WebP with width and height set.

  • GoodSpeed and hosting are fine

    HTTPS, HSTS, the apex-to-www redirect and the Vercel edge cache are all configured correctly. JavaScript is about 400 KB per page uncompressed, worth trimming but not urgent.
